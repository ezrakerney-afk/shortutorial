Watch vs Warning vs Advisory

Your phone just buzzed with a weather alert. Here's what each kind means, and exactly what to do when you get one.

Quick guide~5 minSafety
The short version · 30 seconds

Watch means be ready. Warning means act now.

AlertWhat it meansWhat to do
AdvisoryWeather that's a nuisance or could be risky if you're careless (fog, light snow, heat)Take care, allow extra time
WatchConditions are right for dangerous weather, but it isn't happening yetGet ready: plan, charge phones, know where you'd shelter
WarningDangerous weather is happening or about to happen near youAct now: follow your plan and take shelter

These are the National Weather Service (NWS) terms used across the US. A good memory trick: a watch is when you watch the sky; a warning is when you move.

ACTWhat to do for each alert

Open the one that matches your alert.

Watch: decide where you'll shelter, keep your phone charged, and keep shoes and a flashlight handy.

Warning: go immediately to the lowest floor, into a small interior room without windows (bathroom, closet, hallway). Cover your head and neck. Mobile homes and cars are not safe: get to a sturdy building.

  • Severe means hail 1 inch or bigger, winds of 58 mph or more, or a tornado.
  • Go indoors and stay away from windows. “When thunder roars, go indoors.”
  • Bring in or tie down outdoor furniture and park the car under cover if you can.
  • Move to higher ground if you're in a low-lying area.
  • Turn around, don't drown: never drive or walk into floodwater. Six inches of moving water can knock you over; a foot can float a car.

Watch: hurricane conditions are possible, usually within 48 hours. Stock water, food, medicine and batteries; fill the car's tank; check your evacuation route.

Warning: hurricane conditions are expected, usually within 36 hours. Finish preparations and leave if local officials tell you to evacuate.

The category only measures wind. Storm surge and flooding often cause more harm, so follow evacuation orders even for a “weak” storm.

  • Winter storm warning: stay off the roads if you can; keep a blanket, charger and snacks in the car if you must drive.
  • Extreme heat warning: drink water, stay somewhere cool during the afternoon, and check on older neighbours.
  • Extreme cold warning: cover skin outdoors, and never run a generator or grill indoors (carbon monoxide).

Most phones get Wireless Emergency Alerts for warnings automatically. Check they're on:

  • iPhone: Settings › Notifications › scroll to the bottom › Emergency Alerts and Public Safety Alerts.
  • Android: Settings › Safety & emergency (or Notifications) › Wireless emergency alerts.

For watches and advisories too, use a weather app with alerts turned on, or a NOAA Weather Radio.

FAQCommon questions

Which is worse, a watch or a warning?

A warning. It means the dangerous weather is happening or about to. A watch means it's possible.

What's a “special weather statement”?

A heads-up about something notable that doesn't meet the level of an advisory, like a strong storm that isn't quite severe.

Why did my phone blare a loud alarm?

That's a Wireless Emergency Alert, sent for the most serious warnings (like tornadoes and flash floods) to every phone in the area.

Is a Category 1 hurricane safe to ride out?

Not necessarily. Flooding and storm surge don't depend on the category. Always follow local evacuation orders.
