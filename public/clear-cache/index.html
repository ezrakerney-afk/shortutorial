How to Clear Cache and Cookies

A website looks broken, won't load, or keeps showing old info? Clearing your browser's cache and cookies fixes it surprisingly often. Here's how in every major browser, and what you'll lose before you click.

How-to + fix~5 minEasy

Fastest way · 30 seconds

Open the “clear data” window in one go

Press Ctrl + Shift + Delete on Windows, or ⌘ Cmd + Shift + Delete on a Mac. Or click the ⋮ menu at the top right and choose Delete browsing data. Pick a Time range, tick Cached images and files and Cookies and other site data, then click Delete data.

Press Ctrl + Shift + Delete on Windows, or ⌘ Cmd + Shift + Delete on a Mac. Or go to ⋯ Settings and more › Settings › Privacy, search, and services › Clear browsing data and click Choose what to clear. Pick a Time range, tick Cookies and other site data and Cached images and files, then click Clear now.

Safari bundles things together. Choose History › Clear History… in the menu bar, pick how far back, and click Clear History. That removes your history plus the cookies and cached website data for that period. To clear only cookies, or only the cache, use steps 2 and 3 below.

Press Ctrl + Shift + Delete on Windows, or ⌘ Cmd + Shift + Delete on a Mac. Or click the ☰ menu › History › Clear Recent History…. Set When to Everything, tick Cookies and site data and Temporary cached files and pages, then click Clear.

Then close and reopen the browser and try the website again. Not sure what to tick? Use the checker below first.

101Cache, cookies and history: what's the difference?

🗂️ Cache

Copies of pictures, code and pages your browser saved so sites load faster next time. Nothing personal. Clearing it is safe; pages just load a little slower once. An old or damaged copy is a common cause of a page looking broken.

🍪 Cookies and site data

Small files websites save to remember you: that you're signed in, what's in your cart, your language. Clearing them signs you out of most websites and resets their settings.

🕘 History

The list of pages you've visited and files you've downloaded. Clearing it removes the list and address-bar suggestions. Your bookmarks and downloaded files stay.

🔑 Passwords and autofill

Saved passwords, addresses and cards. These are separate boxes in the clear-data window. Leave them unticked unless you're sure; clearing passwords can't be undone.

Signed in to the browser? If Chrome or Edge sync is on, clearing history and other synced data while signed in can also clear it on your other devices. Edge's help suggests turning off sync first if you only want to clear this computer.

      FAQCommon questions

      Will clearing my cache delete my passwords or bookmarks?

      No. The cache only holds copies of website files. Bookmarks are never touched, and passwords are only deleted if you tick the separate Passwords box.

      Why was I signed out of everything?

      Cookies are what keep you signed in. Clearing them signs you out. Next time, clear only the cache, or clear cookies for one site.

      How often should I clear my cache?

      You don't need to on a schedule. Browsers manage the cache themselves. Clear it when a site misbehaves, or when you're finished on a shared or public computer.

      Does clearing cookies stop tracking?

      It removes the trackers saved so far, but new ones come back as you browse. Your browser's privacy settings, or a private window, do more to limit tracking.

      Does a private or Incognito window hide me completely?

      No. It just doesn't save history, cookies or form data on your computer after you close it. Your internet provider, employer or school, and the websites you visit can still see your activity.

      I cleared my history on my computer. Is it gone from my phone too?

      It can be. If you're signed in with sync on (Chrome, Edge, Firefox) or use iCloud for Safari, clearing history can also clear it on your other devices. For your phone, see clear cache on iPhone and Android.
