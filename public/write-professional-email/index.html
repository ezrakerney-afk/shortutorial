Write a Professional Email

Busy people skim. A good email gets read, understood and answered on the first try. Here's a simple structure that works for almost anything.

01

A subject line that says what it is

The subject line decides whether your email gets opened now or later. Make it specific, and say if you need something.

VagueClear
QuestionQuestion about the March invoice
MeetingCan we move Thursday's meeting to 3 pm?
Hi!Introduction: Sam Lee, design role referral
UpdateProject Atlas: launch moved to May 12
02

Get to the point

  1. Greeting: “Hi Alex,” works for most workplaces; “Dear Ms Patel,” for formal or first contact.
  2. Purpose in the first line: “I'm writing to ask whether…” Don't make the reader hunt for it.
  3. Details: only what they need. Short paragraphs; bullet points for several items.
  4. Clear ask and deadline: “Could you confirm by Friday?”
  5. Sign-off: “Thanks,” or “Best regards,” and your name.

Five sentences or fewer is a good target for most emails. If it's longer, consider a call or a document.

03

Tone: polite, direct, human

  • Swap hedging for clarity: “Just wondering if maybe…” → “Could you…?”
  • Avoid ALL CAPS and lots of exclamation marks; they read as shouting.
  • Reread before sending: would you be happy if it were forwarded?
  • Upset? Write it, then wait an hour before sending.
04

Following up

No reply after a few working days? A short, friendly nudge is normal: reply to your original email so the context is right there.

Example follow-up
  1. Hi Alex,
  2. Just bringing this back to the top of your inbox. Could you let me know about the March invoice by Friday?
  3. Thanks,
    Sam
