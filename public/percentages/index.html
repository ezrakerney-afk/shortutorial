Percentages Made Easy

Discounts, tips, test scores, interest rates: percentages are everywhere. Four short lessons take you from “what does % mean?” to working them out in your head.

01

What a percent really is

“Percent” means per hundred. 25% is 25 out of every 100, the same as the fraction 25/100 or the decimal 0.25.

To turn a percent into a decimal, divide by 100: move the decimal point two places left.

PercentDecimalFractionThink of it as
50%0.51/2half
25%0.251/4a quarter
10%0.11/10a tenth
1%0.011/100a hundredth
200%22/1double

Over 100% is fine. 150% just means one and a half times the original amount.

02

Finding a percent of a number

percent ÷ 100 × numberor: convert the percent to a decimal and multiply
Example · 20% of 85
  1. 20% as a decimal is 0.20
  2. 0.20 × 85 = 17
  3. 20% of 85 is 17

Do it in your head with the 10% trick. To find 10%, move the decimal point one place left. Everything else builds from that.

  • 10% of 85 = 8.5
  • 5% is half of that = 4.25
  • 20% is double = 17
  • 15% is 10% + 5% = 12.75

Handy flip: x% of y is the same as y% of x. 8% of 50 is hard; 50% of 8 is 4. Same answer.

03

Percent increase and decrease

(new − old) ÷ old × 100positive = increase, negative = decrease
Example · rent goes from $1,200 to $1,320
  1. Change: 1,320 − 1,200 = 120
  2. Divide by the old value: 120 ÷ 1,200 = 0.1
  3. × 100 = 10
  4. A 10% increase

Watch out: a 50% drop followed by a 50% rise does not get you back to where you started. $100 → $50 → $75. The second change is a percent of a smaller number.

Percent vs percentage points: if an interest rate goes from 4% to 5%, that's a rise of 1 percentage point, but a 25% increase in the rate itself.

04

Discounts, tax and tips

The quickest way to apply a percent change is to multiply by a single number:

  • 30% off: you pay 70%, so multiply by 0.70.
  • 8% tax on top: you pay 108%, so multiply by 1.08.
  • 18% tip: multiply the bill by 0.18 for the tip, or by 1.18 for the total.

Stacked discounts don't add up. “20% off, plus an extra 10% off” is 0.8 × 0.9 = 0.72, so 28% off in total, not 30%.
