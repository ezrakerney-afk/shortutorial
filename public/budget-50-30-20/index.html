Make a Budget with the 50/30/20 Rule

Budgeting doesn't need spreadsheets with 40 categories. The 50/30/20 rule splits your money three ways and takes ten minutes to set up.

01

The three buckets

Start with your take-home pay: what lands in your account after tax. Then split it:

BucketShareWhat goes in it
Needs50%Rent or mortgage, utilities, groceries, insurance, transport to work, minimum debt payments
Wants30%Eating out, streaming, hobbies, holidays, clothes beyond the basics
Savings and debt20%Emergency fund, retirement, investing, paying off debt faster than the minimum
02

Needs vs wants

A need is something you'd be in trouble without. A want makes life nicer. The basic version of something can be a need while the upgrade is a want:

  • Groceries are a need; takeaway is a want.
  • A phone plan is a need; the newest phone is a want.
  • Getting to work is a need; a fancier car than you require is partly a want.

Be honest, not harsh. The point isn't to cut every want. It's to choose them on purpose.

03

Find out where your money goes

  1. Download the last two or three months of bank and card statements.
  2. Mark each payment as Need, Want or Savings. Your banking app may already group spending by category.
  3. Add up each bucket and compare it with 50/30/20.

Pay yourself first. Set up an automatic transfer to savings on payday. What you never see, you don't miss.

Start an emergency fund before anything else in the savings bucket: aim for one month of expenses, building up to three to six.

04

Make it fit your life

50/30/20 is a starting point, not a law. In an expensive city, needs may take 60% or more; then trim wants to keep saving something. If you have high-interest debt, push extra into the 20%.
