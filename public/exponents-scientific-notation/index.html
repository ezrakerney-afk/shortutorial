Exponents and Scientific Notation

Exponents are a shorthand for repeated multiplication, and they're how scientists write numbers like the distance to the Sun without a page of zeros.

01

What a power means

25 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 32the small number (exponent) says how many times to multiply the big number (base) by itself
  • Anything to the power 1 is itself: 7¹ = 7.
  • Anything (except 0) to the power 0 is 1: 7⁰ = 1.
  • Negative powers mean “one over”: 2⁻³ = 1/2³ = 1/8.
  • Powers of 10 just add zeros: 10⁴ = 10,000.
02

The rules that save time

RuleExample
Multiply same base: add exponents2³ × 2⁴ = 2⁷
Divide same base: subtract exponents5⁶ ÷ 5² = 5⁴
Power of a power: multiply exponents(3²)³ = 3⁶

Careful: 2³ × 3⁴ can't be combined that way. The bases are different.

03

Scientific notation

Write a number as a number between 1 and 10 times a power of 10.

NormalScientific notation
150,000,000 km (Earth to Sun)1.5 × 10⁸ km
8,200,000,000 (people on Earth, roughly)8.2 × 10⁹
0.000001 m (a micrometre)1 × 10⁻⁶ m

The exponent counts how many places the decimal point moved: left for big numbers (positive), right for small ones (negative). Calculators show it as 1.5E8.
