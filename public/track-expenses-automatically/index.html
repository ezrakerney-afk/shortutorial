Track Your Spending Automatically

Writing down every purchase is tedious, so most people give up. Here's how to let your bank and apps do the tracking, and spend just 10 minutes a week on it.

Learn~30 min setupEasy
The big idea · 1 minute

Let your bank do the typing

You don't need to write down every purchase. Pay for almost everything by card or phone, and your bank already records it. The trick is to get those transactions sorted into categories automatically, then look at the totals once a week.

SET UPSet up automatic tracking

Pick the option that suits you, then build the weekly habit.

Most banking apps now group spending into categories like groceries, eating out and bills automatically, with monthly charts. Look for Insights, Spending or Budget in the app. It's free, already connected and the safest option.

Fix any wrongly categorised transactions when you spot them; most apps learn from your corrections.

If you have several banks or cards, a budgeting app can pull them into one view and categorise everything automatically. Popular options include YNAB, Monarch Money, Rocket Money and Copilot (most charge a subscription); many banks also offer free tools.

  • Choose one that connects through a well-known service such as Plaid, and check its security page.
  • Turn on rules, so a merchant is always put in the same category.
  • Set a monthly limit for your two or three biggest categories.

If you'd rather not connect apps to your bank, download your transactions each month as a CSV file (look for Export or Download in online banking) and paste them into a spreadsheet.

  1. Add a Category column.
  2. Use a lookup table of merchant names and categories so most rows fill themselves in.
  3. Insert a pivot table, or a SUMIF formula, to total each category.

The CFPB's free spending tracker shows sensible categories to start with.

  • Cash: withdraw it as one transaction categorised as “Cash”, or note big cash purchases in your phone's notes.
  • Subscriptions: search your statements for recurring charges. Your bank app or phone may list them: on iPhone, Settings › your name › Subscriptions; on Android, the Play Store's Payments & subscriptions.

Automation records spending; looking at it is what changes habits. Once a week:

  • Fix any wrong categories.
  • Compare your top categories with last month.
  • Pick one small change for the coming week.

Spending on autopilot.

Give it a month before setting tight budgets; you'll learn a lot just from seeing the real numbers.

Not sure which to choose?

Start with your bank's app this week. Move to a budgeting app or spreadsheet only if you need to combine several accounts.

FAQCommon questions

Is it safe to connect a budgeting app to my bank?

Reputable apps use read-only connections through services like Plaid, so they can see transactions but not move money. Use strong passwords and two-step verification, and only choose well-known apps.

What categories should I use?

Keep it simple: housing, bills and utilities, groceries, eating out, transport, shopping, subscriptions, health, and savings. Fewer categories means less fiddling.

What happened to Mint?

Intuit shut down Mint in 2024 and moved users to Credit Karma, which offers fewer budgeting features. Many former users switched to the apps above.

Do I have to track every penny?

No. Seeing roughly where your money goes is what matters. Most people find two or three categories cause most of the surprise.
