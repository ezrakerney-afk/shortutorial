Learn Basic Spanish

Spanish is one of the easiest languages for English speakers to start. In six short lessons you'll learn to pronounce it, say hello, count to twenty, get by on a trip, and use the two words for “to be”.

01

Pronunciation: say what you see

Good news: Spanish is spelled almost exactly how it sounds. Learn a handful of rules and you can read any word out loud, even if you don't know what it means.

The five vowels never change

English vowels change sound all the time (think “cat”, “car”, “cake”). Spanish vowels don't. Keep them short and crisp:

LetterSounds likeExample
a“ah” in fathercasa (house)
e“eh” in betmesa (table)
i“ee” in seesí (yes)
o“oh” in go, but shortertodo (all)
u“oo” in fooduno (one)

Consonants that surprise English speakers

  • h is always silent: hola is “OH-lah”.
  • j, and g before e or i, sound like a breathy English “h”: jugo (juice), gente (people).
  • ñ sounds like the “ny” in canyon: año (year).
  • ll and y sound like the “y” in yes in most places: llamo, yo. (In parts of Argentina and Uruguay they sound more like “sh”.)
  • r is a quick tap of the tongue, like the “tt” in American butter: pero (but). rr, or an r at the start of a word, is rolled: perro (dog).
  • z, and c before e or i, sound like “s” in Latin America and like “th” in most of Spain: gracias. Both are correct.
  • qu is a plain “k”: queso (cheese) is “KEH-so”. v sounds almost the same as b.

Where to put the stress

  • Word ends in a vowel, n or s? Stress the second-to-last syllable: CA-sa, HA-blan.
  • Ends in any other consonant? Stress the last syllable: ha-BLAR, ciu-DAD.
  • An accent mark overrides both rules: ca-FÉ, te-LÉ-fo-no.

Accents can change meaning. Si means “if”, sí means “yes”. El is “the”, él is “he”.

Tú or usted? Spanish has two words for “you”. Use tú with friends, family, children and people your age in casual settings. Use usted to be polite: with strangers, older people, customers and bosses. If you're not sure, start with usted. People will tell you if they'd like you to switch.

Spelling tip: questions and exclamations open with an upside-down mark: ¿Qué tal? ¡Hola! It tells the reader early how to say the sentence.

03

Numbers 1 to 20

Tap any number to hear it. Notice that 16 to 19 are built like “ten and six”: dieci-séis.

  • Uno shortens to un before a masculine noun and becomes una before a feminine one: un café, una cerveza.
  • Prices and phone numbers come up constantly. Practice by reading license plates or price tags out loud in Spanish.

05

Masculine, feminine and the word “the”

Every Spanish noun is either masculine or feminine. It's grammar, not meaning: a table (la mesa) isn't female. The gender decides which word for “the” and “a” you use.

MasculineFeminine
the (one)el librola casa
the (more than one)los libroslas casas
a / anun librouna casa
someunos librosunas casas
  • Most nouns ending in -o are masculine; most ending in -a are feminine.
  • Words ending in -ción, -sión and -dad are feminine: la estación, la ciudad.
  • Common exceptions to memorize: el día (day), el mapa, el problema, la mano (hand).
  • To make a plural, add -s after a vowel and -es after a consonant: ciudad → ciudades.
  • Adjectives usually come after the noun and match it: el gato negro, la casa blanca, las casas blancas.

Learn nouns with their article. Don't memorize “mesa”; memorize “la mesa”. Our flashcards do this for you.

06

Ser and estar: two ways to say “to be”

English has one verb “to be”. Spanish has two. Roughly: ser is for what something is, and estar is for how or where something is right now.

Ser · identity

yo soy · tú eres · él/ella/usted es · nosotros somos · ellos/ustedes son

Who or what you are, where you're from, jobs, descriptions, time and dates.

Soy de Texas. I'm from Texas.
Ella es doctora. She's a doctor.
Son las tres. It's three o'clock.

Estar · state and place

yo estoy · tú estás · él/ella/usted está · nosotros estamos · ellos/ustedes están

Location, feelings, health, and temporary conditions.

Estoy cansado. I'm tired.
¿Dónde está el hotel? Where is the hotel?
La sopa está fría. The soup is cold.

Some adjectives change meaning: Es aburrido means “he's boring”, but Está aburrido means “he's bored”.

Memory trick: “How you feel and where you are, always use the verb estar.” One catch: events use ser for location: La fiesta es en mi casa.

You'll also see vosotros (you all, informal) in Spain: sois, estáis. Latin America uses ustedes for every “you all”, which is what most US Spanish speakers use too.

FAQCommon questions

Should I learn Spain Spanish or Latin American Spanish?

Learn whichever you'll use most. In the US, Mexican and other Latin American Spanish are by far the most common. The differences are mostly accent and some vocabulary, like the “th” sound for z and the vosotros form in Spain. Speakers from everywhere understand each other easily.

How long does it take to learn Spanish?

The US Foreign Service Institute puts Spanish in its easiest group (Category I) for English speakers, at about 30 weeks of full-time class (600 to 750 class hours) to reach professional working ability. You can handle greetings, ordering and directions after a few weeks of short daily practice.

Why does the audio sound robotic, or not play at all?

The audio uses the voices built into your device. Most phones and computers include at least one Spanish voice; some need you to download one in your system's language or speech settings. If no Spanish voice is installed, the page tells you, and you can still use the cards silently.

What's the fastest way to remember vocabulary?

Short, spaced reviews beat long cram sessions. Five to ten minutes a day with flashcards, saying each word out loud, works better than an hour once a week. Mark cards honestly so the hard ones come back.

Do I need to learn verb conjugations right away?

Not all of them. Learn ser, estar, tener (to have) and querer (to want) in the present tense first. With those and the phrases above you can say a lot.
