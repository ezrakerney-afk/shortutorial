Time Zones Made Simple

Booking a call with someone abroad, catching a flight, watching a live event: here's how time zones actually work, and how to never miss a meeting again.

01

UTC and offsets

The world sets its clocks relative to UTC (Coordinated Universal Time), essentially the time at 0° longitude, near London. Each place has an offset: how many hours ahead (+) or behind (−) UTC it is.

PlaceStandard offset
Los AngelesUTC−8
New YorkUTC−5
LondonUTC+0
Paris, BerlinUTC+1
IndiaUTC+5:30
TokyoUTC+9
SydneyUTC+10

Not every offset is a whole hour: India is +5:30 and Nepal +5:45.

02

Daylight saving time

Many countries move their clocks forward an hour in summer (“spring forward, fall back”). So New York is UTC−5 in winter but UTC−4 in summer.

The tricky weeks: the US and Europe change on different dates, so for a few weeks each spring and autumn the gap between New York and London is 4 hours instead of 5.

The southern hemisphere does it the other way round: Sydney's clocks go forward in October.

03

Scheduling across time zones

  • When you write a time for others, always include the zone: “3 pm ET / 8 pm UK”.
  • Calendar invites adjust automatically to each person's zone. Let the calendar do the maths.
  • Crossing the International Date Line (in the Pacific) changes the date: flying west you skip a day; flying east you live one twice.
