Negative Numbers

Temperatures below zero, bank overdrafts, floors below ground: negative numbers make sense once you can picture them. Here's the picture.

01

Think of a number line

Negative numbers are numbers below zero. On a number line they sit to the left of zero; the further left, the smaller. So −8 is less than −2, just as −8 °C is colder than −2 °C.

Adding a positive number moves you right. Subtracting moves you left.

Example · −3 + 5
  1. Start at −3
  2. Move 5 to the right
  3. 2
Example · 2 − 6
  1. Start at 2
  2. Move 6 to the left
  3. −4
02

Why minus a minus is a plus

Adding a negative is the same as subtracting: 5 + (−3) = 5 − 3 = 2.

Subtracting a negative is the same as adding: 5 − (−3) = 5 + 3 = 8.

Picture it with debt: if someone takes away a $3 debt from you, you're $3 better off. Taking away a negative is a gain.

Multiplying and dividing

Same signs give a positive; different signs give a negative. The same rule works for division: −20 ÷ −5 = 4, and −20 ÷ 5 = −4.

Counting negatives: with several numbers, an even number of negatives gives a positive answer; an odd number gives a negative one.
