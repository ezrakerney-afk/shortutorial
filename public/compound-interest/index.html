Compound Interest Explained

Interest that earns interest. It's how small savings become large ones over time, and how credit card debt spirals. See it happen with your own numbers.

01

Simple vs compound interest

Simple interest is paid only on the money you put in. Compound interest is also paid on the interest you've already earned, so it snowballs.

YearSimple 10% on $1,000Compound 10% on $1,000
0$1,000$1,000
1$1,100$1,100
2$1,200$1,210
5$1,500$1,611
10$2,000$2,594
20$3,000$6,727

For the first couple of years they look almost the same. Then compound pulls away dramatically.

02

The formula

A = P × (1 + r/n)n×tA = final amount · P = starting amount · r = yearly rate as a decimal · n = times compounded per year · t = years
Example · $5,000 at 5% a year, compounded monthly, for 10 years
  1. r = 0.05, n = 12, t = 10
  2. 1 + 0.05/12 = 1.0041667
  3. 1.0041667120 ≈ 1.647
  4. 5,000 × 1.647
  5. ≈ $8,235

More frequent compounding helps a little, but time and the rate matter far more.

03

The rule of 72

A quick way to estimate how long it takes money to double:

years to double ≈ 72 ÷ interest rate
RateDoubles in about
2%36 years
4%18 years
6%12 years
8%9 years
24% (credit card)3 years

Start early. At 6%, $1,000 doubles roughly every 12 years. Invested at 25, it's about $16,000 by 73. Wait until 37 and it's only $8,000: one fewer doubling.

04

Compounding works against you on debt

Credit cards compound too, often at 20–30% a year. An unpaid $2,000 balance at 24% grows to about $2,540 in one year if you pay nothing, and roughly doubles in three.

Minimum payments trap: paying only the minimum mostly covers interest, so the balance barely shrinks. Paying off high-interest debt is like earning a guaranteed 20%+ return.
