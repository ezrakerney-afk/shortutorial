Piano for Absolute Beginners

Never touched a piano? Start here. In six short lessons you'll find your way around the keys, sit and hold your hands properly, read your first notes, play a real song and learn three chords that unlock hundreds more. Every example works on the piano right on this page.

01

Find middle C

A full piano has 88 keys: 52 white and 36 black. It looks overwhelming, but it's one small pattern repeated over and over.

Look at the black keys. They come in groups of two and groups of three. That pattern is your map.

  • The white key just to the left of any group of two black keys is a C.
  • Middle C is the C closest to the middle of the keyboard. On most pianos and keyboards it's roughly under the brand name or the music stand.
  • On an 88-key piano it's the fourth C from the left, which is why it's also called C4.

On a smaller keyboard (61 or 76 keys), just count the Cs from the left and use the one nearest the center.

02

Name every key

White keys use the first seven letters of the alphabet: C D E F G A B, then back to C. From one C to the next is called an octave, and the higher C sounds like the same note, just higher.

  • D sits between the two black keys. F is just left of the group of three. Use those as extra landmarks.
  • Black keys are sharps (♯) and flats (♭). The black key just right of C is C♯ (“C sharp”), and it's also D♭ (“D flat”): one key, two names.
  • Moving to the very next key, black or white, is a half step. Two half steps make a whole step.
  • There's no black key between E and F or between B and C. Those pairs are already a half step apart.

03

Sit right and number your fingers

  • Sit centered on middle C, far enough back that your elbows are a little in front of your body and roughly level with the keys.
  • Curve your fingers as if you're holding a small ball or an orange. Play on the fleshy pads near the fingertips, not the flat of the finger.
  • Keep your wrists level and relaxed: not drooping, not arched up. Shoulders down.

Piano music labels fingers with numbers, and it's the same on both hands: thumb = 1, index = 2, middle = 3, ring = 4, pinky = 5.

C position: your home base

Put your right thumb (1) on middle C. Let fingers 2, 3, 4 and 5 rest on D, E, F and G, one finger per white key. For the left hand, put your left pinky (5) on the C below middle C, with fingers 4 to 1 on D, E, F and G. Many beginner songs never leave this position.

Practice tip: 10 to 15 minutes a day beats one long session a week. Play slowly enough that you don't make mistakes; speed comes on its own.

04

Read your first five notes

Sheet music for the right hand uses the treble clef. Here are the five notes of C position, exactly where your right-hand fingers sit:

  • Middle C sits on its own short line (a ledger line) below the staff.
  • D hangs just under the bottom line. E is on the bottom line, F in the first space and G on the second line.
  • Notes that go up the staff go right on the keyboard.

06

Your first three chords: C, F and G

A chord is three or more notes played together. The simplest kind, a triad, is built by playing one key, skipping one white key, playing the next, skipping one, and playing the next.

ChordNotesRight-hand fingers
C majorC · E · G1 · 3 · 5
F majorF · A · C1 · 3 · 5
G majorG · B · D1 · 3 · 5

These three chords are the I, IV and V chords in the key of C. Together they can accompany a huge number of folk, pop, country and children's songs.

The easy way: barely move your hand

Jumping between chords is hard at first. Pianists rearrange the same notes so the hand hardly moves. Keep your thumb near middle C:

  • C: C E G, fingers 1 3 5.
  • F: C F A, fingers 1 4 5. Your thumb stays on C.
  • G: B D G, fingers 1 2 5. Your thumb slides down to the B just below middle C.
Try it · chords
Pick a chord to see and hear it.

Next step: play the chord with your right hand while your left hand plays just the chord's name note (C, F or G) low on the keyboard. That's how a lot of real accompaniment sounds.

FAQCommon questions

Do I need a real piano to start?

No. A keyboard is fine for learning. If you're buying one, look for at least 61 full-size keys; 88 weighted or hammer-action keys feel most like a real piano and make switching later easier. A sustain pedal is a cheap, useful extra.

How long until I can play a song?

Many beginners can play a simple melody like Ode to Joy in the first day or two. Playing melody and chords together usually takes a few weeks of short daily practice.

Why is middle C called C4?

Notes are numbered by octave, counting from the bottom of the piano. The lowest C on an 88-key piano is C1, so middle C is the fourth C: C4. The A just above it, A4, is the 440 Hz note orchestras tune to.

The on-screen piano is silent. What's wrong?

Browsers only allow sound after you tap or click the page. Tap a key once. On iPhone, also check that the ring/silent switch isn't on silent, and that your volume is up.

Should I learn by ear, apps, or with a teacher?

All work. A teacher catches bad habits early, like flat fingers or tense wrists, that are hard to notice on your own. If you learn alone, film your hands now and then and compare with the hand-position tips above.
