NFL Overtime Rules, Explained

Tied at the end of regulation? Here's exactly what happens next, in the regular season and the playoffs.

Quick guide~3 min2026 rules
The short version · 30 seconds

Both teams get the ball, then next score wins

Since 2025, the regular season uses the same “both teams get the ball” rule the playoffs use. Before that, a touchdown on the first drive ended regular-season games straight away.

HOWHow an overtime plays out

Follow along step by step.

The visiting team's captain calls the toss. The winner usually chooses to receive or kick off. (Many coaches now choose to kick, so they know exactly what they need when their turn comes.)

The receiving team drives. Whatever happens (touchdown, field goal, or nothing), the other team then gets its own possession.

The one exception: if the kicking team's defense scores a safety on that first drive, the kicking team wins immediately.

  • If they score more than the first team did, they win.
  • If they score less (or nothing), the first team wins.
  • If they match it, the game goes to sudden death.

Matching a touchdown means teams often go for two-point conversions to settle it.

After both teams have had the ball, the next score of any kind wins: a field goal, touchdown or safety.

In the regular season, if the 10 minutes run out first, the game ends in a tie. In the playoffs, they keep playing more 15-minute periods.

FAQCommon questions

Can an NFL game end in a tie?

Yes, in the regular season, if it's still tied after the 10-minute overtime. Ties are rare but happen. Playoff games can't tie.

Does a field goal on the first drive win?

No. The other team always gets the ball and can win with a touchdown or tie with a field goal.

How do ties count in the standings?

A tie counts as half a win and half a loss when working out winning percentage.

Is college football overtime the same?

No. College teams alternate possessions starting from the opponent's 25-yard line, and from the third overtime they run two-point attempts only.
