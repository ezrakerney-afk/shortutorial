Fractions in 10 Minutes

Recipes, measurements, splitting a bill: fractions turn up all the time. Here's everything you need, one short step at a time.

01

What a fraction means

A fraction is a number of equal parts. The bottom number (the denominator) says how many parts make a whole. The top number (the numerator) says how many parts you have.

A fraction is also a division: 3/4 means 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75.

02

Equivalent fractions and simplifying

Multiply or divide the top and bottom by the same number and the fraction keeps its value: 1/2 = 2/4 = 50/100.

To simplify, divide top and bottom by the biggest number that goes into both (the greatest common factor).

Example · simplify 18/24
  1. Both 18 and 24 divide by 6
  2. 18 ÷ 6 = 3 and 24 ÷ 6 = 4
  3. 18/24 = 3/4

Not sure of the biggest factor? Divide by any shared number, like 2 or 3, and repeat until nothing else goes into both.

03

Adding and subtracting

You can only add or subtract fractions when the bottoms match. If they don't, rewrite them with a common denominator first.

Example · 1/3 + 1/4
  1. A number both 3 and 4 go into: 12
  2. 1/3 = 4/12 and 1/4 = 3/12
  3. Add the tops: 4 + 3 = 7
  4. 1/3 + 1/4 = 7/12

Common mistake: adding the bottoms too. 1/3 + 1/4 is not 2/7; that's smaller than either of them.

Mixed numbers like 2½ are easiest as improper fractions: 2½ = 5/2 (2 × 2 + 1 = 5 halves).

04

Multiplying and dividing

Multiply: top × top, bottom × bottom. No common denominator needed.

2/3 × 3/5 = 6/15 = 2/5

Divide: flip the second fraction and multiply. (“Keep, change, flip.”)

3/4 ÷ 1/2 = 3/4 × 2/1 = 6/4 = 1½how many halves fit into three-quarters? One and a half
